Three small kiosks lost 85% of their gross margin to waste.

Kudapan Nusantara had ten outlets and only a monthly revenue report. I built a Power BI dashboard to show which outlets really earn money.

A solo project in Power BI on simulated sales and waste data, January 2024 to June 2025. The 10 outlets are 7 stores (standalone stores) and 3 kiosks (small booths inside a mall or market), around Greater Jakarta.

Across 10 outlets, Rp 95.7M of gross margin was earned and Rp 27.7M of it was lost to waste, 28.9%. Press a button to look at kiosks and stores separately.

The dry season

Sales jump from May and peak at Rp 6.1M in July 2024 and Rp 6.9M in May 2025. The pattern also holds per outlet, so it is not only the new outlets opening.

What to do first

1

Fix cold storage at the three kiosks

Their revenue is healthy. Waste takes 79% to 93% of their gross margin. If they lost margin at the stores' rate of 13%, they would have kept about Rp 15M more over the 18 months. That is an upper bound, not a forecast.

2

Order less ice cream in the dry season, until then

If budget is tight, cut ice cream and popsicle stock at those three outlets from May to September, before the freezer investment is done.

Skip

The seven stores

They have medium or large freezers and waste takes 6% to 22% of their margin. That is a reasonable level, so no extra investment is needed.

The dashboard behind it

Two pages: Overview compares all ten outlets, and Outlet Deep Dive opens one outlet chosen with a slicer.

The badges on the cards change text, color and icon from DAX measures, so they follow the outlet being viewed. Chart interactions are set by hand on the Overview, so clicking a slicer does not change the general recommendation panel.

How I built it

1

Model the data

A star schema: sales and waste as two fact tables, joined to outlet, product and calendar dimensions.

2

Clean it

Filled the blank unit cost of two products with their category average, merged 8 spellings of the category field into 4, and set aside 2 sales rows with impossible dates. Negative quantities are kept, because they are returns.

3

Value waste at cost

Waste is priced at what the product cost, not its selling price, so the number is money actually lost rather than sales that might have happened.

4

Rank and check

Ranks and company averages use RANKX and AVERAGEX over all outlets, so a slicer cannot move the benchmark. Every figure was checked against a DAX query before it went on the dashboard.

What it can't tell you

Cold storage is the leading explanation for the kiosks, not a proven cause.

Every kiosk has a small freezer and no store does, so this data cannot separate freezer size from the kiosk format itself, such as mall foot traffic or opening hours.

Waste has only been recorded since January 2024, so there is nothing earlier to compare with.

Outlets opened at different times, from June 2023 to January 2025. Newer outlets have fewer months of revenue, so totals favor the older ones.

The data has no cost for a freezer upgrade, so the dashboard cannot say how much a fix is worth paying for.

All data is simulated for this portfolio. The methods carry over; the figures do not describe a real company.
