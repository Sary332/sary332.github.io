01

SaaS · Subscription churn

SQL Server

Over half of Flowdesk's cancellations followed a failed payment.

Three teams blamed three different causes for rising churn: failed payments, Paid Ads customers and slow support. I tested each one against the same data in SQL, with confidence intervals. One held up clearly, one held only for the entry tier, and one showed no effect.

56%
of cancellations followed a failed payment (257 of 460)
38x
higher cancel rate after a failed payment than after a successful one
3
theories tested, with a 95% interval on each

The question

Three teams blamed three different causes for rising churn. Which one does the data actually support, and what should be fixed first?

What I did

  • Cleaned six tables: removed 11 duplicate payments, unified channel names and rebuilt six missing cancel dates.
  • Defined an active subscription by start and end dates, so every past month stays accurate.
  • Tested each theory with a rate and a 95% confidence interval, leaving out customers too new to judge.

02

IT support · Operations

Power BI DAX

Ticket volume more than doubled once the enterprise clients arrived.

Leadership could fund three new support hires. The Power BI dashboard showed the SLA problem was not everywhere: Tier 1 was getting slower and the night shift answered last, while the team that missed its SLA most had the happiest customers.

+123%
ticket volume after enterprise onboarding
+49%
Tier 1 resolution time, from 83 to 124 hours
21.3 h
first response on the night shift, with only 2 agents

The question

Leadership could fund three support hires. Which team or shift would they help most?

What I did

  • Built a Power BI star schema: one ticket fact table joined to agent, category, SLA and date dimensions.
  • Cleaned the data: removed 18 duplicate rows, merged 30 category spellings into 7 and fixed a timezone bug.
  • Wrote DAX time intelligence on fixed date ranges for before and after, and tested the SLA breach definition two ways.

03

Retail F&B · Outlet performance

Power BI DAX

Three small kiosks lost 85% of their gross margin to waste.

Kudapan Nusantara had ten outlets and only a monthly revenue report. The Power BI dashboard showed that three kiosks with small freezers lost most of their margin to waste, while the seven stores kept a net margin near 40%.

85%
of gross margin lost to waste at the 3 kiosks
9.7%
net margin of the outlet ranked 4th on revenue, against 29.5% on average
28.9%
of company gross margin lost to waste

The question

Which outlets really earn money once waste is counted, and where should the company act first?

What I did

  • Modeled sales and waste as two fact tables in a Power BI star schema.
  • Valued waste at cost, so the number is money actually lost rather than sales that might have happened.
  • Ranked outlets with RANKX and AVERAGEX so a slicer cannot move the benchmark, and checked every figure with a DAX query.

04

Logistics · Delivery operations

SQL Server

Bandung had five drivers for 1,076 orders, and 59% of its deliveries missed the SLA.

A courier startup could not say if late deliveries came from a city, the drivers or unrealistic contracts. In SQL Server I found Bandung short of drivers and motorbikes failing on heavy parcels. I also caught and fixed a mistake in how I first measured lateness.

215
orders per driver in Bandung, against 104 to 134 elsewhere
59%
of Bandung deliveries missed the SLA, against 21% to 25% elsewhere
81% to 30%
overall breach rate after I corrected my own SLA clock

The question

Are late deliveries caused by a city, the drivers or unrealistic merchant SLAs, and where should new drivers go?

What I did

  • Cleaned four tables in T-SQL: removed 69 duplicate rows, folded 13 city spellings into 4 and flagged 538 backfilled promised dates.
  • Caught that my first SLA clock started at the order date, and corrected it, which cut the breach rate from 81% to 30%.
  • Tested whether one merchant's lead survived without the rows I had filled in. It did not.
