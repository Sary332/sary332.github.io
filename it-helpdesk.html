Where the three hires go

1

Tier 1

The only team getting slower, up 49% since onboarding. It is the biggest bottleneck today, so capacity goes here first.

2

Night shift, if any are left

First responses take 21.3 hours against 11.6 to 11.8 on the other shifts, with only 2 agents on duty.

Skip

Tier 2

The most stable and fastest team. Its high reopen rate on software bugs is about how tickets are handled, not how many people are available.

The dashboard behind it

Two pages: Overview for the condition of the team and the hiring call, Deep dive for the causes.

The date slicer on each page is deliberately not synced, because the two pages answer different questions. Chart interactions are set by hand so a click on one visual cannot change the intended reading of another.

How I built it

1

Model the data

A star schema: one ticket fact table joined to agent, category, SLA, and date dimensions.

2

Clean it

Removed 18 duplicate rows from a system migration, merged 30 spellings of the category field into 7, fixed a timezone bug in resolution dates, and left tickets without a survey response out of the CSAT average.

3

Compare before and after

DAX time intelligence locked to fixed date ranges, so March 2026 onward is always measured against the months before it, even when a slicer moves.

4

Test the definition

SLA breach calculated two ways, from first response and from full resolution. Tier 3 breaches most and Tier 2 least under both, so the ranking does not depend on the definition.

What it can't tell you

Read the recommendation as a ranking of need, not a staffing model.

The slowdown lines up with the March 2026 onboarding, but a before and after comparison cannot prove the onboarding caused it.

Only 30% of tickets carry a survey response (1,777 of 5,915), so CSAT reflects the customers who chose to answer.

Sizing the hires properly needs cost and tickets per agent, which are not in this data. The analysis says where, not how many.

All data is simulated for this portfolio. The methods carry over; the figures do not describe a real company.
